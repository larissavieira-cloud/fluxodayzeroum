Reproduce each one exactly — same species, same coat, plumage or scale pattern, same colour, same horn, beak, ear and eye shape, same garment cut and colour, same mark in the same position at the same size. Do not restyle, do not redesign, do not stylise, do not anthropomorphise, do not give it a human face or human hands, do not change the species, do not move or duplicate the mark, do not swap the garment.
Those inputs are REFERENCE SHEETS on a white background: never reproduce the sheet grid, the multiple panels, the turnaround views, the close-up insets or the white backdrop. Take only the subject out of them. Exactly 2 animals, one per character input in frame. Each stays its own species and its own garment — never merged, never duplicated, never swapped.
Fur, feather and scale detail resolve at photographic level: individual fibres and barbs, natural clumping and direction, moisture in the eye, texture in the beak, hoof or claw, and the everyday grime of the place sitting on top.

ANATOMY LOCK — absolute, overrides everything else
The anatomy comes from the input and nothing else. Never add a human limb or a human feature of any kind: no human arms, no human hands, no fingers, no opposable thumbs, no human legs, no human feet, no human face, no human torso. The limb count is exactly what the reference shows. Joint direction, bone length and body plan stay correct for the real species. Paws stay paws, hooves stay hooves, wings stay wings, talons stay talons, the muzzle or beak keeps its real shape, the tail keeps its real length and set.
Every action in this photograph is performed with the animal's own body — hoof, wing, beak, claw, snout, shoulder, head, tail. If an action would need a hand, change the action, never the anatomy. If a human appears in frame, it is a separate person, clearly human, and never a hybrid.

SPECIES BEHAVIOUR AND SUPPORT
Read the species from each input and place it the way that species actually exists in the world. A perching bird is perched — on a rail, a chair back, a rope, a post, a branch or a shoulder — with its talons wrapped around the support and its weight settled onto it, never standing flat on an open floor. A hoofed or four-legged animal stands on all four legs on ground that could carry its weight. A climbing reptile grips a horizontal surface with its claws splayed and its belly low to it. Whatever the species does in real life is what it does here: where its body can physically be, what it needs to hold on to, how it carries its weight, how it rests, how it turns its head to look. Never place an animal somewhere its real body could not support it, and never invent a support that species would not use. Where more than one species is in frame, each follows its own rule independently — they do not share a stance.

POSTURE
Keep exactly the body plan and posture shown in the reference: if it stands upright on its hind legs it stays upright, if it is a natural quadruped it stays on four legs. Do not re-rig, do not humanise, do not straighten the spine, do not add or remove limbs.

SCALE
Each animal is at the true physical size of its real species, measured against the doors, furniture, railings, bottles, phones and people in frame. If a person appears, the size relationship must be anatomically correct. No giant version, no miniature version.

INTEGRATION — what makes it one photograph
The animal and the environment are one photograph, not two layers. Feet, hooves, claws or talons physically press into the surface they stand on, compressing grass, denting cushion, sitting flat on wet concrete. A short dark contact shadow sits directly under every contact point, spreading and softening away from it. At least one foreground element crosses in front of the body and partly hides it. The scene light hits the animal from the same direction, at the same colour temperature and the same hardness as everything else in frame, and the ambient bounce of the floor tints the underside. The room is reflected in the moisture of the eye and in any leather, metal or glossy fabric. Airborne dust, moisture or grease from this place settles on the coat, feathers or scales. Depth of field, grain and sharpness on the animal are identical to the background — never crisper, never cleaner.

LOCATION
stadium terrace at night, crowd rows behind, floodlight towers above, LED perimeter glow, wet concrete steps. Real place with real clutter: crumpled plastic cups on the step, a folded scarf over the rail, phone cables, someone half in frame at the edge. Mixed colour temperature from the sources that actually exist here.

INTERACTION
The animals are crowded in together on the same support, one leaning across the other so their shoulders overlap and press; the nearer one has its head tipped up toward the camera mid-call with its mouth open; the other is turned away looking off past the lens at something in the background, gripping the support to hold its place. Someone out of frame is leaning in beside them.

GARMENT AND BRANDING
The garment stays exactly as the reference shows it — same cut, same colour, same mark in the same place. The garment has been worn all day: wrinkle at the elbow and waist, collar slightly turned, a little dust or sweat darkening, one seam pulling where the body moves.
Four zeroum marks in the environment as real manufactured objects: a crimson #B4263C vinyl banner with brass grommets and a wind crease, a sticker on a bottle or laptop with a lifting corner, a printed A4 sheet taped to a wall with the tape visibly yellowing, and a branded cup or bottle among the clutter. Every environmental mark is a real manufactured object: screen print raised slightly on the surface, vinyl with a matte edge, anodised enamel with a specular line, printed webbing, banner with grommets. Each follows the curve or fold of what it sits on, is lit by the scene's own light, and shows wear. Never a flat digital overlay, never a floating logo, never a watermark.

LIGHT

HIERARCHY — fixed, never negotiated
RENDER TIER: AA+ photographic realism, captured and not rendered. No element steps below this tier, and no element is finished to a lower standard than the one in front of it.
READ ORDER: 1 the animals · 2 their contact points and shadows · 3 foreground clutter · 4 midground dressing · 5 background. Nothing swaps rank, nothing ties for first.
DETAIL BUDGET: hero detail on rank 1 only — individual fibres, barbs or scales, moisture in the eye, texture in the beak, hoof or claw, and the grime of the place on top. Each lower rank drops one step. The last two ranks read by shape and value alone.
LUMINANCE RAMP: minimum 5 discrete depth planes, each one stop darker and one step less saturated than the plane in front of it. State the ramp.
LIGHT UNITY: one master key for the whole frame. Every element obeys the same azimuth, elevation and colour temperature. Coloured shadows, never neutral grey.
OBJECT LAW: every element is a physical object in the world — real thickness, a visible mount or contact point, and a cast shadow on whatever sits behind it. Nothing floats, nothing is pasted, nothing is an overlay.
MONTAGE LAW: adjacent ranks overlap so depth is evidenced and never merely implied. Nothing sits fully isolated in clean air. At least one foreground element crosses the frame edge, cropped.

CAPTURE
24mm main-camera equivalent, mild wide-angle perspective, slight facial enlargement when close, deep depth of field with the background still legible. Night mode multi-frame capture: elevated shadow detail with visible chroma noise, slight ghosting on anything that moved during the exposure, halation blooming around every light source, warm-shifted white balance. Shot by a bystander standing close: framing off-centre and a little crooked at 3 degrees, one subject partly clipped by the frame edge, nobody fully composed, focus landed on the nearer face. Neutral-to-cool white balance with the phone's own tone curve, minimal vignette, specular highlights clipping slightly on wet and metal surfaces. No colour grade applied afterwards.

9 zeroum marks distributed across depth: 2 hero marks embossed, forged, stitched or printed at readable scale on the largest surfaces; the rest smaller and partly occluded — on fabric, panels, crates, glass and floor. Every mark obeys the surface it sits on: it curves with cloth, wraps a cylinder, sits flat on plastic, and catches the scene key. Never a flat digital logo overlay, never a watermark, never floating. The reference labels themselves are never rendered as text in the image.

HIERARCHY — fixed, never negotiated
RENDER TIER: AA+ Disney/Pixar theatrical quality, stylized cartoon 3D forms with photoreal material behaviour. No element steps below this tier, and no element is finished to a lower standard than the one in front of it.
READ ORDER: 1 hero element · 2 headline object · 3 supporting assets · 4 foreground props · 5 midground dressing · 6 background. Nothing swaps rank, nothing ties for first.
DETAIL BUDGET: hero render on rank 1 only — individual fur strands with subsurface scattering, wet specular in the eyes, fabric weave and stitching, scuffed edges, dust and debris. Each lower rank drops one step of detail. The last two ranks read by shape and value alone.
LUMINANCE RAMP: minimum 5 discrete depth planes, each one stop darker and one step less saturated than the plane in front of it. State the ramp.
LIGHT UNITY: one master key for the whole frame. Every element obeys the same azimuth, elevation and colour temperature. Coloured shadows, never neutral grey.
OBJECT LAW: every element is a physical object in the world — real thickness, a visible mount or contact point, and a cast shadow on whatever sits behind it. Nothing floats, nothing is pasted, nothing is an overlay.
MONTAGE LAW: adjacent ranks overlap so depth is evidenced and never merely implied. Nothing sits fully isolated in clean air. At least one foreground element crosses the frame edge, cropped.

LEGIBILITY — the copy must be effortless to read, this outranks composition
· CAP HEIGHT: headline capitals are at least 7% of frame height, subheadline at least 3.5%. If a string is too long to hold that, break it into more lines — never shrink the letters to fit.
· LINE LOAD: maximum 3 words per line and maximum 3 lines on the headline, maximum 4 words per line on the subheadline.
· CONTRAST: the letter fill differs from the surface directly behind it by at least 40 points of luminance. The plate interior behind the glyphs stays a quiet even value — no busy grain, no pattern, no ornament and no hot highlight crossing a letter.
· CLEAR SPACE: no character, prop, particle, light shaft, rain streak or atmospheric effect crosses any glyph. Margin around the text block is at least one cap height on every side.
· FOCUS: all lettering sits on the focal plane, fully sharp, never softened by depth of field, never motion blurred.
· GEOMETRY: maximum 10 degrees of tilt and minimal perspective foreshortening on the text plane. Letters are never stretched, condensed, warped, or cropped by the frame edge.
If any of this conflicts with the composition, the composition yields. Copy that cannot be read makes the piece worthless.

HEADLINE INTEGRATION — it is BUILT INTO the world, never placed on top of it
· Made from a material already present in this scene, sharing its grain, its wear, its weathering and its colour temperature. It looks like it was made here.
· Physically supported by scene structure that could actually carry that weight — posts under load, ropes under tension, bolts sunk into stone, a beam it hangs from, a wall it is set into. The support is visible and does real work.
· Dressed and framed by props from the world, at least two of them touching or overlapping its frame.
· Lit by the scene key, catching the same highlight as everything around it, dropping its own shadow onto the layer behind, with occlusion where the mount meets it.
· The environment is composed AROUND it: architecture, sightlines and perspective converge on it, and something in the scene passes behind it. It is never a rectangle floating over a finished background, never a sticker, never a pasted plaque.

Por que o papel de cada asset importa

Logo de jogo e foto de prêmio não se tratam como personagem. Logo tem que ser reproduzido pixel a pixel, porque é marca de terceiro e qualquer redesenho vira erro jurídico e visual. Prêmio precisa de proporção e acabamento corretos, senão o carro sai com a forma errada e a peça perde credibilidade. Personagem aceita pose nova. São três travas diferentes, e é o campo de papel que decide qual vale para cada um.
